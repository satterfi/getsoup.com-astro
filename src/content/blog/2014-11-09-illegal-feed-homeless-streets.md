Well, I don't have a clear answer for you.  Why is it illegal to feed homeless?

Could it be money?  Political money that is.  Certainly not money associated with "helping" people in their situation.

## Cost of housing

One of the significant contributors to homelessness is cost of housing.

Consider for a moment, let's say your income is [$10/hour](http://www.ncsl.org/research/labor-and-employment/state-minimum-wage-chart.aspx), and you've been fortunate enough to find two positions where you can work a total of 40 hours per week (25 for one position, and 15 for the other).  Your gross monthly income is about $1700.  Subtract $150 for federal withholding, $100 for social security, $25 for medicare, and $50 for state income tax, and your take home pay is now around [$1350/month](http://www.paycheckcity.com/calculator/salary/).

What can you do with $1350/month?  First lets take out your known expenses:

- You have to eat ($15/day total, including breakfast, lunch, and dinner, to the tune of $450/month.  Do you eat on $15/day?  I'll bet not.),
- you have to keep a reserve to buy clothes (this includes socks, and pants, and shirts, and shoes, and undergarments, say $30/month?)
- you have to launder your clothes and towels and sheets ($4/load plus laundry detergent plus dry time, two loads per week, $32/month)
- you are likely to need either public transportation ([monthly pass in Pittsburgh is $150](http://www.portauthority.org/paac/FaresPasses/Fares.aspx)), or you will need a vehicle to transit to work.

What does that leave?  Just over $650/month.  That includes no going out at all, no movies, no Starbucks, no hair cuts, no McDonald's, no KFC, no unexpected costs, no doctors appointments, no emergency room visits, no dental appointments.

 

 

Let's take a case look at a situation.

## The Miami Homeless Trust

"The Miami-Dade County Homeless Trust (Homeless Trust) serves as the coordinating entity for services for homeless individuals and families throughout Miami-Dade County. The Homeless Trust administers a portion of the one percent Food and Beverage Tax proceeds, as well as federal, state, and other resources dedicated to services for the homeless; advises the Board of County Commissioners on issues related to homelessness; serves as the identified "Lead Agency" for the United States Housing and Urban Development (USHUD) funds for our community, and the State’s Homeless Coalition for Miami-Dade County; implements the Miami-Dade County Community Homeless Plan; and provides administrative, contractual, and policy formulation assistance related to homeless services." ([source](http://www.miamidade.gov/budget/library/FY2013-14/adopted/volume3/homeless-trust.pdf))

### Homeless Trust public revenue

The Homeless Trust FY13/14 revenue was $55 Million.  This is the one year budget, identified in the [FY 2013 - 14 Adopted Budget and Multi-Year Capital Plan](http://www.miamidade.gov/budget/library/FY2013-14/adopted/volume3/homeless-trust.pdf).

### Homeless Trust homeless count

According to the Homeless Trust as reported by [WLRN](http://wlrn.org/post/miami-dade-homeless-count-tallies-350-more-last-year), there are approximately 4000 homeless people in Miami Dade county, with about 3300 of them living in some sort of shelter, some of those shelters being funded by our public taxpayer funded Homeless Trust or by private donations.

### Expenditures per homeless person per year

So even in an extremely myopic funding scenario, taking into account only the Public Funds of $55,000,000 and believing the 3300 homeless are being tended to by that $55,000,000, we wind up with $16,000 being expended per person per year.  In reality, this number is much greater than $16,000 per person, since this does not include private donations and expects that every one of the 3300 are being housed by part of the public trust.

What does $16,000 per person per year buy?  It buys a program.  But oddly enough, it does not buy a home.  It does not guarantee the person is going to be off the streets.

It buys a program.

Wouldn't it be more efficient to provide perpetual housing for every homeless person in Dade County with the $55M, instead of buying  a program?

Just a thought.
